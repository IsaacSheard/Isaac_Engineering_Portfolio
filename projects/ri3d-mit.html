Head Captain: MIT's First Robot in 3 Days

Robot in 3 Days (Ri3D) is a tradition in FIRST Robotics: a team designs and builds a full competition robot in the 72 hours after the season's game is revealed, then publishes everything so FRC teams can learn from it. 2026 was MIT's first Ri3D. As Head Captain, I led the captain team and ~25 MIT undergrads who grew up in FRC. We built a working robot for the 2026 game, REBUILT, within 72 hours of kickoff on January 10, 2026.

01

Standing It Up

The program didn't exist before this year. We started it with a Ri3D president, a group of captains, and ~25 students. As Head Captain, I led the other captains and ran the team through rapid CAD, design reviews, and software development.

I set up the planning meetings with the other captains. Together we worked out what we needed to buy given our budget. I emailed local companies asking for material donations, which got us the sheet metal and plywood below, and I personally fronted about $4,500 for time-critical parts.

02

Design Decisions

On kickoff day we split into groups to break down the game manual, then I led the strategy session on the livestream to set priorities. The must-haves were an L1 climb, a shooter that works without vision, and storage for at least 8 game pieces, with an L3/L2 climb as the stretch goal. We deliberately ruled out vision-based shooting and shooting while climbing. We also built a field bump first so we could test driving over it, and the robot launched off it on the first test.

REBUILT has a lot of scoring elements and no limit on how many a robot can carry, so we designed around capacity:

  • Drivetrain: swerve on a 27×27 in square frame, which gives more area than a rectangle of the same perimeter
  • Intake: over-the-bumper, with a box that extends out for extra storage
  • Storage: we prototyped two options, a "Maze" track through the robot and a "Hopper" bucket with an indexing wheel, and picked the slanted Hopper, targeting 30–40 game pieces
  • Shooter: stationary, fixed-angle at the back of the robot. No turret: swerve aims the robot, and a fixed shooter stays consistent without vision
  • Climber: we designed toward a tilted L3 climb as the stretch goal, since L3 is the only way to secure the climb ranking point with partners who can't climb, then scaled back to the must-have L1 climb during the build
  • Electrical: WAGO connectors as CAN hubs, with each swerve module wired separately
03

Results

We had a fully functional robot within 72 hours of kickoff, in the program's first year. The climber was the one thing that didn't hold up: it broke during a climb because some of its rivets hadn't been set properly. We published the build as we went: daily updates on Chief Delphi, livestreams on Twitch and YouTube, and the full CAD and code.

After the build, we kept improving the robot and brought it to the inaugural 2026 U.S. Governor's Cup in Washington, DC, where our team ran it as a student-operated demo on a REBUILT field.
