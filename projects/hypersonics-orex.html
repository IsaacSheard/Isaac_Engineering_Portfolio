Predicting Reentry Blackout Around OREX

Hypersonics researcher on a reentry plasma modeling project that aims to predict electron density and radio-communication blackout around the OREX spacecraft using US3D CFD.

The Problem

During reentry, the shock layer around a vehicle gets hot enough to ionize the air, and the resulting plasma can block radio signals. This is the communications blackout. The project's goal is to predict electron density around OREX, and from it the blackout, using US3D CFD.

02

What I Built

Geometry. OREX is a 50° sphere-cone: a 1350 mm nose radius, a 3400 mm diameter, and a 100 mm shoulder radius. I reconstructed the vehicle profile from JAXA's published layout drawing and NASA's dimensioned geometry, and verified the dimensions against published data.

Grid. I'm building a structured axisymmetric grid in Pointwise for the US3D simulations. Because OREX is axisymmetric, a 2D grid captures the forebody flow at a fraction of the cost of a full 3D mesh. Cells are packed toward the wall to resolve the thin shock layer, where the plasma forms.

I check quality with equiangle skewness, where 0 is a perfect cell and 1 is fully degenerate. The grid stays low across the nose and forebody, where the flow matters most, and rises toward the shoulder and outer boundary.
