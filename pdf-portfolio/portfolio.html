MIT Rocket Team · Fall 2025

Carbon Fiber
Motor Case FEA

Taught myself Ansys ACP to size the team's filament-wound CFRP case for a factor of safety of 2.

Result
~8 layers for FOS 2 (hand calcs; FEA consistent)
Material
T800S/epoxy, 60% Vf, orthotropic
Tools
Ansys ACP (Pre), Static Structural, Modal, Composite Failure Tool

Model

Symmetric ±54° plies (near the 54.7° netting angle) on a ~10 mm shell mesh, under internal pressure.

Iterations

Fibers weren't following the winding path because ACP saw 3 rosettes where I'd defined 1. The Winding Wizard (geodesic layup) fixed coverage.

Catching bad results

Our spec-sheet material pinned SF at 1000 even at 1000× pressure. Since it didn't change with load, the model was wrong, so I proved the pipeline on a known material first.

Outcome

We couldn't get oven access to cure the case before go/no-go, so carbon fiber moves to a future motor. The model is ready when it does.

MIT Hypersonics Lab · Sep 2026 – Present

Reentry Blackout
Around OREX

Predicting electron density and radio blackout around JAXA's OREX reentry capsule with US3D CFD.

Role
Hypersonics researcher
Vehicle
OREX, a 50° sphere-cone
Tools
US3D, Pointwise, Tecplot

The problem

During reentry the shock layer gets hot enough to ionize the air, and the plasma blocks radio: the communications blackout. The goal is to predict electron density around OREX, and from it the blackout.

Geometry

Reconstructed the vehicle from JAXA's layout drawing and NASA's dimensioned geometry: RN 1350 mm nose, ø3400 mm body, Rc 100 mm shoulder. Verified against published data.

Grid

Building a structured axisymmetric grid in Pointwise, packed toward the wall to resolve the thin shock layer. Equiangle skewness stays low across the nose and forebody and rises toward the shoulder.

Design, Build, Fly:
5th Place

Sized, analyzed, built, and flew a competition RC aircraft with a four-person team, trading flight speed against how far payload CG could shift aft while staying controllable.

Role
Aerodynamics & stability analysis; wing mount & assembly
Team
4 · "The Millennium Falcons"
Tools
Vortex Lattice Method, ScanARS sizing code

Approach

I ran a closed-loop iteration between a VLM aero solver and ScanARS until the wing converged: AR 8.69, S = 0.177 m², b = 1.24 m. A washout study (+2° root, −3° tip) smoothed the spanwise lift dip to reduce tip-stall risk. We picked a 21.6% static margin, just above the recommended band, to buy a larger safe aft-payload CG range.

Build

Built and installed the wing mount, aligned the servo/mount assembly, trimmed the fuselage to design geometry, and balanced CG to match the analysis.

Result

As-built geometry matched spec. It flew at 11.09 m/s with a 0.404 payload fraction in both nominal and aft-CG configurations, placing 5th overall.

JetJoe Turbojet:
Model vs. Test

Predicted a JJ-1400 turbojet's thrust, mass flow, and turbine temperature from first principles, then built and live-fired the engine to see where the model held up.

Role
Cycle modeling, thrust/efficiency analysis, assembly & test
Team
4 · "The Millennium Falcons"
Methods
Brayton cycle, control-volume momentum, thrust stand

What I did

Derived the engine thrust equation from a control-volume momentum analysis, worked the specific-thrust and SFC derivations, and solved for turbine inlet temperature from a compressor–turbine shaft-work balance. Measured blade angles and throat area directly off the hardware, and ran the coast-down test that verified shaft alignment before live fire.

Result

Clean live fire to ~160,000 rpm. The idealized model under-predicted real performance, and the gap traced back to an optimistic 70% compressor-efficiency assumption.

Star Trek Adventure:
2nd Place

Solo-designed, built, and programmed a rover for a head-to-head, 3-minute gameboard competition where autonomous manipulation scores double.

Role
Individual: strategy, mechanism, chassis, autonomy
Tools
Onshape, line sensor, IMU, wheel encoders
Result
2nd overall, $125 prize

Strategy from numbers

Before building, I modeled every challenge's manual, autonomous-nav, and fully autonomous point value. Shuttle (1,880 pts) and Tricorder (1,820 pts) dominated, so I built a "Shuttle-First" route and set time budgets of 15 s and 20 s from tile-per-second math.

Hardware

Designed one motor mechanism to drive both the shuttle crank and tricorder dial at a fixed height. Redesigned the chassis to four-wheel drive with rear-mounted electronics for more predictable autonomous turns.

Testing

Bench testing showed the crank took ~4 min 50 s against a 15 s budget: geometrically right, badly underpowered.
